An **Action Taken Report (ATR)** is a report in which an authority records **what steps it has taken on a complaint or direction**. The term is used widely in government grievance systems, vigilance and Lokayukta complaints, court-directed enquiries and police petitions. In a police station context, an ATR (or a "status report") tells the complainant or the court what the police did after the complaint was received.

## Where you will meet an ATR

- **Police petitions.** When a complaint is given to a police station or a senior officer, the officer may call for an ATR from the station on what action was taken.
- **Courts.** The High Court or a Magistrate may ask the police or an authority to file an ATR on the steps taken in compliance with an order.
- **Grievance portals.** On central and state grievance portals, the department closes a grievance by uploading an ATR.
- **Vigilance and Lokayukta complaints.** The authority forwards the complaint to the department and asks for an ATR.

## What a good ATR should contain

- The **reference number** of the complaint and the date received.
- A summary of the **allegation** being examined.
- The **steps taken**: enquiry, statements recorded, documents collected, registration of an FIR or reasons for not registering.
- The **outcome**, with reasons, and the date.
- The **name and designation** of the officer who prepared it.

## What the law says about informing the complainant

Under the **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**, the police must **inform the informant or victim about the progress of the investigation within ninety days** and keep them informed thereafter, including by electronic communication. When a complaint discloses a cognizable offence, the police are expected to register an FIR. For certain offences with punishment of three to seven years, the police may conduct a **preliminary enquiry** of up to fourteen days with the permission of a senior officer. These provisions help a complainant to ask for a written account of what has been done.

If the police file a **closure report** saying no offence is made out, the Magistrate must give the informant notice and an opportunity to object, usually through a **protest petition**. The Supreme Court established this safeguard in *Bhagwant Singh v. Commissioner of Police* (1985).

## How to follow up if you do not get an ATR

1. **Keep the acknowledgement.** At a Chennai police station this is generally the receipt for the petition, which is entered in the Community Service Register (CSR). Do not leave without one.
2. **Write to the senior officer.** If no action is taken, send a written complaint with a copy of the receipt to the Deputy Commissioner or Commissioner of Police. Under BNSS Section 173(4), you can approach the Superintendent of Police if the station refuses to register an FIR.
3. **Approach the Magistrate.** If the police still fail to register an FIR, you can apply to the Magistrate under Section 175(3) of the BNSS, with an affidavit, to direct registration and investigation.
4. **Use the Right to Information Act.** Ask for the file notings or status of the complaint, subject to exemptions.
5. **Writ or Section 528 petition.** In appropriate cases, the High Court can direct the police to act or to file a report.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is an ATR the same as a charge sheet?
No. A charge sheet, or final report, is the police report filed in court after investigation. An ATR is a record of the action taken on a complaint or direction.

### Can I get a copy of the ATR?
In many cases, yes. If the ATR was prepared in response to your complaint, you can ask for it, and the Right to Information Act is available where it is denied.

### What if the police say no offence is made out?
Ask for the reasons in writing. You can approach senior officers, the Magistrate or the High Court.

### Does the police have to register an FIR on every complaint?
For a cognizable offence, yes, following *Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh* (2014), subject to a limited preliminary enquiry in specified categories.

### Who prepares an ATR on a court order?
The authority that was directed, such as the police or the concerned department, and the court may ask for it to be sworn.
