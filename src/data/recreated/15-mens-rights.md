Indian law on cruelty, dowry and domestic violence was written mainly to protect women, and the statutes are drafted around a woman complainant. That does not leave a man without rights. A man facing a complaint is protected by the **same constitutional and procedural safeguards as any accused person**, and the courts have laid down specific guidelines against automatic arrest and misuse. This page summarises those safeguards.

## The laws most often involved

- **Cruelty by a husband or his relatives** under Sections 85 and 86 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (earlier Section 498A of the IPC).
- **Dowry offences** under the Dowry Prohibition Act, 1961.
- **Domestic violence** proceedings under the Protection of Women from Domestic Violence Act, 2005, which are civil in nature, with some criminal consequences for breach of orders.
- **Maintenance** under Section 144 of the BNSS (earlier Section 125 CrPC) and under personal laws.

## Safeguards against arrest

In *Arnesh Kumar v. State of Bihar* (2014), the Supreme Court directed that in offences punishable with imprisonment of up to seven years, **the police must not arrest automatically**. They must be satisfied that arrest is necessary under the statutory criteria and record reasons, and issue a **notice of appearance** instead where arrest is not necessary. Magistrates must examine whether the police have complied before authorising detention. These principles continue to apply under the BNSS.

## Remedies when a complaint is false or exaggerated

1. **Anticipatory bail** before arrest, from the Sessions Court or the High Court.
2. **Quashing** of the FIR where allegations are vague, omnibus or against distant relatives without specific acts. The Supreme Court has repeatedly cautioned against the misuse of these provisions and has quashed cases that involved general allegations against family members.
3. **Discharge** at the charge stage, if the material does not disclose the offence.
4. **Defending the domestic violence case**, by filing a reply with documents showing income, residence and conduct.
5. **Action against a false complaint**, where the facts support it, including proceedings for a false charge under the BNS. This is a serious step and needs strong evidence.

## Maintenance and custody

Maintenance is decided on the **income and needs of both spouses**. A working spouse's earnings are considered, and the Supreme Court has directed that both parties file **affidavits of assets and income** in maintenance cases (*Rajnesh v. Neha*, 2020). A wife living in adultery is not entitled to maintenance, though this must be proved. In custody disputes, the court's governing principle is the **welfare of the child**, not the gender of the parent, and fathers can claim custody and visitation.

## Evidence that helps

- Bank statements, income-tax returns and proof of payments made.
- Messages, emails and recordings that are lawfully obtained and admissible.
- Medical records, photographs and the names of independent witnesses.
- A clear timeline of events, with dates.

## Practical steps

- **Do not ignore** a notice or summons.
- **Do not contact the complainant** in a way that could be portrayed as pressure.
- Take advice on **mediation**: courts often refer matrimonial disputes for settlement, and a genuine settlement can end the case, with the High Court able to quash the proceedings where appropriate.
- Keep all communications calm and in writing.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a man file a domestic violence case?
The 2005 Act covers women. A man who is a victim of violence can use general criminal law, such as the provisions on hurt and intimidation.

### Is 498A non-bailable?
It is a cognizable and non-bailable offence, but bail and anticipatory bail are regularly considered, and arrest is not automatic.

### Can the whole family be named?
Complainants sometimes name relatives, but courts expect specific allegations and quash proceedings where they are omnibus.

### Does a settlement end the case?
For offences like these, the High Court can quash on a genuine settlement, though it is not automatic.

### Will I lose my children?
Custody is decided on the child's welfare. Taking prompt legal advice helps protect visitation and contact.
