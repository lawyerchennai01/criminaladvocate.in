A traffic challan in Chennai is either an **e-challan** that can be paid online, or a **court challan** that requires you to appear before a Magistrate. Which one you get depends on the offence. Minor violations such as helmet or signal offences are usually settled by paying a fine, while serious offences, such as drunk driving, dangerous driving or driving without a licence, can require a court appearance.

## Types of traffic challans

| Type | Usual cases | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Spot or e-challan** | Helmet, seat belt, signal jumping, parking, speeding captured on camera | Pay online or at the designated counters within the stated period |
| **Court challan** | Drunk driving, driving without licence or insurance, rash driving, unpaid or contested challans | Appear before the Metropolitan Magistrate on the date given |

## How to check and pay an e-challan

- Use the **Parivahan** portal or the official Chennai traffic police e-challan service, with the vehicle number or challan number.
- Pay within the stated period to avoid it being sent to the court.
- Keep the **payment receipt**, since it is the proof that the matter is closed.

If a challan is unpaid, the matter can be forwarded to the court, and you may receive a **summons or notice**. Ignoring it can lead to a warrant and additional trouble when you renew your licence or sell the vehicle.

## What happens in the court

1. **Appearance.** You appear on the date given. The court reads the allegation to you.
2. **Plea.** You may plead guilty and pay the fine, or contest the charge.
3. **Evidence.** If you contest, the police witness is examined, and you can cross-examine and lead your own evidence.
4. **Order.** The Magistrate imposes the fine or other penalty, or acquits you.

Fines depend on the offence as set out in the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988, and its 2019 amendments. For some offences, the court can also order **suspension of the licence**.

## Settling cases through compounding and Lok Adalat

Many minor traffic offences can be **compounded**, meaning the offender pays a prescribed amount and the case ends, without a trial. Courts also hold **Lok Adalats**, where pending and pre-litigation traffic challans can be settled, often with a reduced fine. Check the eCourts services and the District Legal Services Authority for the dates of such events in Chennai.

## When to consider a lawyer

- The challan is for a **serious offence** with possible imprisonment or licence suspension.
- You **dispute the facts**, such as the vehicle number, identity, speed or location.
- Several challans have accumulated and you need to **resolve them together**.
- Your **licence or vehicle has been impounded**, and you need to recover it.

## Practical tips

- Always take a **receipt** for any document or licence handed over to the police.
- **Do not ignore** a notice, because the consequences can multiply.
- **Do not pay agents or offer money**. Offering a bribe is a separate offence.
- Check that the **vehicle number and the location** in the challan are correct.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I have to go to court for every challan?
No. Most e-challans can be paid online. A court appearance is needed when the challan is a court challan or has been referred to court.

### Can my licence be suspended for traffic challans?
For certain offences and for repeated violations, the authorities or the court can suspend or disqualify a licence.

### What if I was not driving the vehicle?
Inform the court or the authority and provide details of who was driving. The registered owner may be asked to explain.

### Is there a time limit to pay?
A period is stated in the challan. After it expires, additional steps can follow.

### Can I contest an e-challan generated by camera?
Yes, if there is a mistake. Follow the dispute procedure on the portal, or raise it in court.
