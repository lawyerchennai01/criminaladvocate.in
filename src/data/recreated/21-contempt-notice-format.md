When a person or authority does not comply with a court order, the party who obtained the order can take two steps: first, send a **notice demanding compliance**, and then, if there is no response, file a **contempt petition** in the court. This page gives the usual format of both documents, and explains how to respond if you receive a show-cause notice. The contempt law is the **Contempt of Courts Act, 1971**, read with Articles 129 and 215 of the Constitution.

## A notice before filing is advisable, not mandatory

The Act does not require a notice before a contempt petition, but courts like to see that the other side was **made aware of the order** and given a reasonable chance to comply. A notice also helps to prove that the disobedience was **wilful**.

## Format of a demand notice

**Heading:** "Notice demanding compliance with the order dated ___ passed by the Hon'ble High Court of Madras in W.P. / C.S. / Crl.O.P. No. ___ of ___."

**Sent to:** the person or officer responsible for compliance, at the official address, by registered post, speed post and email, with proof of delivery.

**Body:**

1. **Introduction.** State who you are and in which case the order was passed.
2. **The order.** Quote the operative part of the order and attach a **certified copy**.
3. **Knowledge.** State that the order was communicated or served on a specified date.
4. **Non-compliance.** Describe exactly what has not been done and the date by which it was required.
5. **Demand.** Ask for compliance within a stated period, such as seven or ten days.
6. **Consequence.** State that if there is no compliance, contempt proceedings will be filed, and costs will be claimed.
7. **Signature.** Signed by the party or the advocate.

## Outline of a civil contempt petition in the Madras High Court

1. **Cause title:** "In the High Court of Judicature at Madras, Contempt Petition (Civil) No. ___ of 2026, in W.P. No. ___ of ___."
2. **Heading:** "Petition filed under Sections 11 and 12 of the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971 and Article 215 of the Constitution of India to punish the respondent for wilful disobedience of the order dated ___."
3. **Affidavit and facts:** the case history, the order, the date of service, the steps taken to obtain compliance, and why non-compliance is wilful.
4. **Grounds:** the order was clear; the respondent knew of it; there was no impossibility; the respondent had the capacity to comply; and the delay is deliberate.
5. **Prayer:** to take action against the respondent for contempt and to direct compliance.
6. **Annexures:** certified copy of the order, proof of service, the demand notice and the reply, if any.

Remember the **one-year limitation** in Section 20 of the Act, counted from the date of the alleged contempt.

## If you receive a show-cause notice

- **Read the order** said to be violated and understand its exact terms.
- **File a detailed response** with an affidavit on what was done and when. If compliance has been completed, attach proof.
- **Explain any inability** to comply, such as an unclear direction, a pending appeal or a stay order. Wilfulness is essential for contempt.
- **Consider a sincere apology** where there was a lapse. Courts may accept a *bona fide* apology.
- **Appear on the date given.** Failing to appear can lead to a coercive process.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a notice required before filing a contempt petition?
No, but it is good practice, and courts often expect that the other side was given an opportunity to comply.

### Who is liable in the case of a government department?
The officer responsible for compliance may be called to appear. Courts focus on the officer who had the duty to implement the order.

### Can I file a contempt petition if there is an appeal pending?
Yes, unless the order has been stayed. A stay or the pendency of an appeal without stay does not suspend the order.

### What is the difference between contempt and execution?
Execution enforces the decree by process of the court. Contempt punishes disobedience to maintain the authority of the court.

### What punishment can follow?
Simple imprisonment up to six months, a fine up to ₹2,000, or both, unless the court accepts an apology.
