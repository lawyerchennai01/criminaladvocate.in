Money should not stand in the way of a person in a rape case, whether they are a **survivor** or an **accused**. Indian law provides **free legal aid** and **victim compensation**, and a private advocate's fee is a matter of agreement. This page explains how costs and aid work. It does not quote any fees, because they differ from advocate to advocate and case to case.

## For survivors

- **Free legal aid.** Under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987, women and children are entitled to free legal services irrespective of their income. A survivor can apply to the **District Legal Services Authority (DLSA)** in Chennai or ask the police or the Magistrate to arrange a lawyer.
- **Support during the case.** Police stations and courts are expected to inform a survivor of the right to a legal aid advocate. Many One Stop Centres provide counselling and referral.
- **Compensation.** The victim compensation scheme (Section 396 of the BNSS, earlier Section 357A CrPC) and the State's scheme for victims of sexual offences provide **interim and final compensation**, which is decided by the legal services authority or the court, and does not depend on the outcome of the trial.
- **Privacy.** Hearings are held in camera, and publishing the identity of the survivor is an offence.
- **Private counsel.** A survivor can also engage a private lawyer, and in many cases a **victim's advocate** can assist the prosecutor with the court's permission.

## For a person accused

An accused is also entitled to a lawyer. The Constitution (Article 39A) and the Legal Services Authorities Act provide free legal aid for those who cannot afford one. If the accused is in custody and unrepresented, the **Magistrate must inform them of the right to a legal aid advocate**, and the court appoints one if the accused is not represented at trial. The legal aid advocate must give the same care as a private one.

## If you engage a private advocate

- **Written agreement.** Ask for the fee and scope in writing: what the fee covers (bail, trial, appeal), and what is extra.
- **Receipts.** Take a receipt for each payment.
- **No promises.** The Bar Council of India's rules prohibit an advocate from guaranteeing a result or charging a fee contingent on the outcome.
- **Expenses.** Clarify who bears the expenses for certified copies, travel and experts.
- **Sensitivity.** In these cases, how the advocate treats the person is important. Ask how they will handle the survivor's evidence respectfully, or how they will explain the process to the accused and family.

## Factors that make costs higher or lower

- **Stage:** bail, trial before the Sessions or Mahila Court, or appeal.
- **Complexity:** number of witnesses, medical and forensic evidence, and connected proceedings.
- **Court and place:** appearances at the High Court or outside Chennai.
- **Duration:** how many hearings the trial requires.
- **Related cases,** such as POCSO proceedings or a petition to quash.

## Where to seek help in Chennai

- **Tamil Nadu State Legal Services Authority and DLSA, Chennai:** free legal aid.
- **Police emergency number 112** and the **Women Helpline 181.**
- **CHILDLINE 1098** for children.
- **One Stop Centres and the District Social Welfare office** for counselling and shelter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do survivors have to pay a fee to file a complaint?
No. Registering an FIR is free, and a survivor has the right to a copy of the FIR.

### Is a legal aid advocate less capable?
Legal aid advocates are enrolled lawyers who are required to represent their clients diligently. A person who is dissatisfied can ask the authority for a change.

### Can an accused get legal aid?
Yes, if they cannot afford a lawyer, and the court must ensure representation at trial.

### Is compensation paid only if the accused is convicted?
No. In many cases interim compensation can be granted at an early stage, and it does not depend on conviction.

### Can I change my advocate midway?
Yes, but settle the fee and take back your documents, and inform the court with a fresh vakalatnama.
