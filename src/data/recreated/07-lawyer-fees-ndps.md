There is no fixed fee for an NDPS or Essential Commodities (EC) case in Chennai. The cost depends on the **stage** of the case, the **quantity and seriousness** of the allegation, the **courts** involved and the **work** required. This page explains these factors so that you can ask the right questions before engaging an advocate. It deliberately gives no figures, because advocates set their fees individually and a fixed rate would be misleading.

## What the EC and NDPS courts handle

In Chennai, matters under the Narcotic Drugs and Psychotropic Substances Act, 1985 (NDPS Act) and the Essential Commodities Act, 1955 are heard in special courts for these offences. Appeals and bail petitions of a higher kind go to the Madras High Court, and in some cases the Supreme Court.

## Factors that influence the fee

- **Stage of the case:** bail at the investigation stage, trial before the special court, or an appeal each need different work.
- **Quantity involved:** the NDPS Act classifies quantities as small, intermediate or commercial. Commercial quantity cases attract stricter bail conditions under Section 37 and typically need more preparation.
- **Court and location:** a hearing in a distant court or the High Court adds travel and time.
- **Number of hearings and accused:** cases with several accused and witnesses take longer.
- **Seniority and team:** a senior advocate with juniors for research and drafting will charge differently from a solo practitioner.
- **Complexity of evidence:** chemical analysis reports, procedural defects in search and seizure (Sections 42, 50 and 52A), and mandatory compliance questions require detailed examination.
- **Additional proceedings:** quashing petitions, bail cancellation, forfeiture of property under Chapter VA, or detention challenges all add work.

## Bar Council of India rules on fees

The Bar Council of India's rules on professional conduct do not allow an advocate to stipulate a fee *contingent on the result* of the case or to share fees with non-lawyers. A fee can be fixed per appearance, per stage, or as a package, but it should not depend on winning. An advocate must also not promise a particular outcome.

## How to avoid misunderstandings

1. **Ask for a written fee agreement** stating what the fee covers (for example, one bail application, or the trial) and what is extra.
2. **Ask how fees are billed** for adjournments, transfers and appeals.
3. **Get receipts** for every payment.
4. **Clarify who will appear** in court, the senior or a junior.
5. **Discuss expenses** separately: court fee, certified copies, travel and expert opinions.

## Free legal aid

A person accused of an offence who cannot afford a lawyer is entitled to free legal aid under Article 39A of the Constitution and the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. A person in custody can ask the Magistrate to appoint a legal aid advocate, and the District Legal Services Authority in Chennai can be approached directly. Courts also appoint an advocate when an accused is not represented at trial.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a government-fixed fee for NDPS cases?
No. Fees are agreed between the advocate and the client, except for legal aid advocates, who are paid by the legal services authority.

### Do fees differ for small and commercial quantity cases?
Commonly yes, because commercial quantity cases involve stricter bail conditions and longer trials.

### Can I pay in instalments?
That is a matter between you and your advocate. Ask for the arrangement to be recorded in writing.

### Does a higher fee improve the chance of bail?
No. Bail depends on the law, the facts and the court. A fee does not guarantee an outcome, and no advocate should suggest otherwise.

### What if I cannot afford a lawyer?
You can apply for free legal aid through the Legal Services Authority or ask the court to appoint a legal aid advocate.
