People in Chennai search for individual police stations when they need to file a complaint, check on a case or support a family member who has been called in. The stations that appear most often in these searches include **T15 SRMC, E3 Teynampet, T7 Tank Factory, C2 Elephant Gate, H7 Peripheral Hospital, R10 MGR Nagar, S11 Tambaram, K7 ICF, P6 Kodungaiyur, M4 Redhills, K4 Anna Nagar, J1 Saidapet, R5 Virugambakkam and M7 Manali New Town**. The process and your rights are largely the same at every station, and this page explains them.

The station's address, telephone number and **jurisdiction** change from time to time, so this page does not list them. For the exact details of a station, check the official Greater Chennai Police website or the Kaaval Uthavi app, or call the emergency number **112**.

## Which station has jurisdiction?

Each station covers a defined area. A complaint is normally made at the station where the incident took place. If you are unsure, you can approach any station: a **Zero FIR** can be registered at any police station for a cognizable offence and then transferred to the station with jurisdiction (Section 173 of the BNSS).

## Filing a complaint

1. **Write the complaint** with your name, address and phone number, the date, time and place of the incident, a clear description, and the names of the persons involved, if known.
2. **Sign it and keep a copy.** Ask the station to stamp and acknowledge your copy.
3. **Cognizable offence.** For offences such as theft, assault, cheating or serious crimes, the police must register an **FIR**. The Supreme Court held in *Lalita Kumari v. Government of Uttar Pradesh* (2014) that registration is mandatory, with a limited preliminary enquiry in specified categories.
4. **Other complaints.** For less serious matters or disputes, the station may enter the petition in the **Community Service Register (CSR)** and give you a receipt. A CSR is an acknowledgement, not an FIR.
5. **Online options.** Complaints can also be made through the Tamil Nadu Police citizen services portal and the Kaaval Uthavi app, depending on the type of complaint.

## If the station does not register your FIR

- **Write to senior officers.** Send the complaint to the Deputy Commissioner or the Commissioner of Police with a copy of the receipt. Section 173(4) of the BNSS allows you to approach the Superintendent of Police.
- **Approach the Magistrate.** Under Section 175(3) of the BNSS, you can apply to the Magistrate, with an affidavit, to direct the police to register the FIR and investigate.
- **High Court.** In appropriate cases, a petition can be filed in the Madras High Court.

## If you or a family member are called to a police station

- **Ask for the reason** in writing. For offences with imprisonment up to seven years, the police are expected to issue a **notice of appearance** instead of arresting automatically.
- **You have a right to know the grounds of arrest** and to have a relative or friend informed.
- **You have the right to consult a lawyer** (Article 22(1) of the Constitution), including during questioning.
- **Production before a Magistrate** is required within **twenty-four hours** of arrest.
- **Medical examination** can be requested, and any injury or ill-treatment should be reported to the Magistrate.
- **The D.K. Basu guidelines** (Supreme Court, 1997) require an arrest memo with the arrestee's signature, a witness to the arrest and intimation to a family member.
- **Women, children and some other persons** cannot ordinarily be required to attend a police station for questioning; the police should question them at their residence. A woman should not be arrested after sunset and before sunrise, except in exceptional cases with a Magistrate's prior permission.

## When a lawyer can help at a station

A lawyer can accompany you to the station, check that the **procedure is followed**, review the FIR, apply for **anticipatory or regular bail** and file representations to senior officers. They cannot guarantee how the police will act, and you should not pay money to anyone at a station for the registration of a case or for release.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between an FIR and a CSR?
An FIR starts a criminal investigation for a cognizable offence. A CSR receipt only records that a petition was received and enquired into.

### Can I file a complaint at any police station in Chennai?
You can approach any station, and a Zero FIR can be registered for a cognizable offence. It will then be transferred to the correct station.

### Do I have to pay anything to register a complaint?
No. Registration of an FIR is free, and you are entitled to a free copy.

### Can the police detain me without informing my family?
No. The police must inform a relative or friend of your arrest and the place of detention.

### How do I find the contact details of a station?
Use the official Greater Chennai Police website or the Kaaval Uthavi app, or call 112 for emergencies.
