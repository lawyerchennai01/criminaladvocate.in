There is **no fixed fee** for a 498A (now Section 85 and 86 of the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita) dowry harassment case. Advocates set their fees individually, and the total cost depends on **what stage the case is at and how long it runs**. This page explains the factors so that you can plan and ask the right questions. It deliberately gives no amounts, because published figures for a legal matter would mislead.

## Why costs vary so much

A 498A case rarely consists of a single step. A family may need several proceedings over a number of years:

| Stage | Typical work involved |
|---|---|
| Before arrest | Anticipatory bail petition, representation to the police, reply to notices |
| After FIR | Regular bail, quashing petition in the High Court, if grounds exist |
| Investigation | Appearance before the police, joint enquiry in the Mediation Centre, documentation |
| Trial | Charge stage, evidence of several witnesses, cross-examination, final arguments |
| Appeal | Appeal or revision against conviction or acquittal |
| Connected cases | Divorce, maintenance, domestic violence and custody proceedings |

## Factors that influence the fee

- **Number of accused persons** and whether the family needs separate representation.
- **Court and place.** Appearances in the Magistrate court, Sessions Court, High Court or outside Chennai will differ.
- **Length of the trial,** which depends on the number of witnesses and adjournments.
- **Parallel proceedings,** since the marriage dispute often includes matters in the Family Court.
- **Seniority of counsel** and the size of the team.
- **Mediation and settlement.** Genuine settlements can reduce the overall cost but still require drafting and appearances.

## Bar Council rules and fairness

An advocate **may not charge a fee that depends on the result** of the case or promise a particular outcome. A fee may be fixed by stage or by appearance, but it should be **disclosed and agreed in advance**. You are entitled to ask for a **receipt** for every payment, and to be told clearly how fees apply to adjournments and later stages.

## Tips for managing cost

1. **Ask for a written fee agreement** that states what the fee covers (for example, an anticipatory bail petition) and what is excluded.
2. **Ask how fees apply across stages,** so that you are not surprised later.
3. **Plan for related expenses,** such as court fees, certified copies, travel and translations.
4. **Keep your documents organised** so that your advocate's time is used well.
5. **Discuss settlement early,** where it is a realistic option.

## Legal aid

If you cannot afford a lawyer, free legal aid is available to women and to persons below a specified income under the Legal Services Authorities Act, 1987. A person in custody can ask the Magistrate for a legal aid advocate, and the District Legal Services Authority in Chennai can be approached directly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a 498A case a one-time fee?
Usually not. The work extends over several stages, and fees are commonly agreed by stage.

### Does a higher fee improve the outcome?
No. The result depends on law and evidence, and no advocate can guarantee it.

### Will a settlement end the fees?
Settlement reduces ongoing costs but may still involve drafting, mediation and a High Court petition for quashing.

### Can the wife claim litigation costs?
In maintenance and matrimonial proceedings, courts can direct the husband to pay litigation expenses in appropriate cases.

### Are there free alternatives to a private lawyer?
Yes, through legal aid for those who qualify, and through mediation centres attached to courts.
