An **Interpol Red Notice** is an alert circulated to police in member countries asking them to **locate and provisionally arrest** a person wanted for prosecution or to serve a sentence, pending extradition or similar legal action. It is **not** an international arrest warrant. Interpol itself does not arrest anyone: each country decides what action to take under its own laws. In India, the **Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)** is the National Central Bureau that communicates with Interpol.

## Red Notice, Look Out Circular and extradition

| Instrument | Issued by | Effect |
|---|---|---|
| **Red Notice** | Interpol, on request from a member country | A request to locate and provisionally arrest; not binding on any country |
| **Look Out Circular (LOC)** | Indian authorities | Restricts a person from leaving India, or alerts immigration officials |
| **Extradition request** | A State to another State through diplomatic channels | The formal legal process to surrender a person |

Under the Extradition Act, 1962 and treaties with other countries, a person arrested abroad on a Red Notice can contest extradition in that country's courts. The notice alone does not prove guilt.

## How an Indian case reaches a Red Notice

An Indian investigating agency, usually the CBI or a State police unit through the CBI, applies to Interpol for a Red Notice when a person who is wanted in an Indian case is believed to be abroad. The usual prerequisites are a **valid arrest warrant** from an Indian court and a summary of the facts. Individuals cannot "apply" for a Red Notice against another person; only a law-enforcement agency can request one.

## If a Red Notice has been issued against you

- **Do not ignore it.** A person named in a notice may face detention at an airport or arrest in another country.
- **Find out the basis.** Obtain the FIR, charge sheet or warrant that led to the request.
- **Challenge the case in India.** Quashing, bail, or recall of the warrant before the Indian court can remove the basis of the notice.
- **Approach Interpol's CCF.** The **Commission for the Control of Files (CCF)** is an independent body that reviews requests from individuals to access, correct or delete data in Interpol's files. The request is made in writing with identity proof and a reasoned explanation, and may be accompanied by court orders.
- **Challenge in the country of arrest.** If you are detained abroad, you can contest extradition and seek bail there.

## Grounds on which Interpol deletes notices

Interpol's rules do not allow notices that are of a **political, military, religious or racial character**, and require that data comply with human rights principles. A notice may also be deleted if the underlying warrant is cancelled, the case is quashed or acquitted, or the request does not meet Interpol's standards.

## Documents usually needed

- Passport and identity details.
- Copy of the FIR, charge sheet, warrant and any court orders.
- Proof that the case has been closed, quashed or stayed, if applicable.
- A written explanation of why the request is unfounded or politically motivated.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can India arrest me just because of a Red Notice from another country?
A Red Notice alone is not a legal basis for arrest in India. Arrest happens under Indian law, usually after a request routed under the Extradition Act.

### Does a Red Notice stop me from travelling?
It can lead to detention at borders in many countries, so travelling while one is pending carries serious risk.

### How long does the CCF take to decide?
Review can take many months. It is advisable to prepare a detailed, documented request.

### Is there an official "application form" for a Red Notice?
No individual application form exists. Requests come from law enforcement agencies. For deletion, the CCF has its own request procedure.

### Who handles Interpol matters in India?
The CBI, which is the National Central Bureau, coordinates with Interpol for Indian cases.
