Driving under the influence of alcohol is an offence under **Section 185 of the Motor Vehicles Act, 1988**. A person commits the offence if they drive, or attempt to drive, with a **blood alcohol content above 30 milligrams per 100 millilitres of blood**, as shown by a breath analyser or a blood test, or are under the influence of a drug to the extent of being incapable of controlling the vehicle. In Chennai these cases are common during police checks, especially at night and on weekends.

## Penalties

| Offence | Punishment under the Act |
|---|---|
| First offence | Imprisonment up to **six months**, or fine up to **₹10,000**, or both |
| Second or later offence within three years | Imprisonment up to **two years**, or fine up to **₹15,000**, or both |

The police can **arrest without a warrant** for this offence. In practice, many first-time cases end with a fine after the court is satisfied about the evidence, but the court has the power to impose imprisonment. Amounts and terms were revised in 2019, so the current text should be checked.

## What happens after you are stopped

1. **Breath test.** The police use a breath analyser. If the reading is over the limit, the officer may record it and the vehicle may be seized.
2. **Alternate tests.** You can ask for a blood test at a hospital, which is a more reliable measure in some circumstances.
3. **Challan or FIR.** The police issue a challan, or in some cases register a case, and your **driving licence or vehicle documents may be impounded**.
4. **Production before the Magistrate.** You are given a date to appear before the Metropolitan Magistrate or traffic court.
5. **Plea and sentence.** You may plead guilty and pay the fine, or contest the case. A conviction can also lead to **suspension or disqualification of the licence** and an endorsement on it.

## When the matter is more serious

If the drunk driving results in an accident, the police can add charges under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for **rash and negligent driving**, causing **hurt or grievous hurt**, or **causing death by negligence**. These carry much higher penalties and a longer legal process, and an insurance claim may also be affected.

## Defences often examined

- **Procedure of the test:** whether the breath analyser was calibrated and used properly, and whether the test was done within a reasonable time.
- **Evidence of the reading:** whether the reading was recorded accurately and the accused was given the printed result.
- **Identity and driving:** whether the accused was actually driving or attempting to drive.
- **Medical explanation:** certain conditions or medicines can affect results.
- **Documentation:** missing or inconsistent challan details and seizure records.
- **Mitigation:** for first-time offenders, steps such as counselling and a clean record can matter at sentencing.

## What to do if you are stopped

- Stay calm and cooperate with the test. Refusing a test can lead to further problems.
- Do not argue with the police or offer money. Offering a bribe is itself an offence.
- Note the officer's name, the reading, and the time and place.
- Ask for a copy of the challan and any receipt for documents seized.
- Appear on the date given. Missing the date can lead to a warrant.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I be arrested for a first drunk driving offence?
The police have the power to arrest, but they usually issue a challan and require you to appear in court.

### Will my licence be cancelled?
The court can suspend or disqualify the licence on conviction. Impounding by the police is a separate, temporary step.

### What is the legal limit?
30 mg of alcohol per 100 ml of blood.

### Can I contest the breath analyser result?
Yes. The test method, the device and the procedure can be questioned in court.

### Does a drunk driving case affect a passport or visa?
A conviction can appear in background checks. Consult an advocate about the effect on travel in your case.
