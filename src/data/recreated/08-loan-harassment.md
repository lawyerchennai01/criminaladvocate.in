If you owe money on a loan or credit card, the lender has the right to recover it, but it has **no right to harass, threaten or humiliate you**. Banks and NBFCs must follow Reserve Bank of India (RBI) guidelines on recovery, and abusive recovery can also be a criminal offence. This page explains your rights and how a settlement should be negotiated and documented.

## What recovery agents may and may not do

Under RBI's fair practices and recovery instructions, regulated lenders and their agents:

- must identify themselves and the lender they represent;
- should contact you only at reasonable hours, ordinarily between 8 am and 7 pm;
- must not use threats, abusive language, or violence, and must not harass you or your family;
- must not contact your friends, relatives, colleagues or employer to shame you into paying; and
- must not publicly display your name as a defaulter in a manner that humiliates you.

A lender remains responsible for the conduct of the recovery agents it appoints.

## Remedies for harassment

1. **Complaint to the lender.** Write to the bank's grievance officer and keep a copy. Regulated entities must respond within a fixed time.
2. **RBI Integrated Ombudsman.** If the lender does not resolve the complaint, you can complain to the RBI Ombudsman, free of cost, through the RBI's complaint portal.
3. **Police complaint.** Threats, intimidation, stalking, extortion or defamation by recovery agents can be reported to the police. The Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita covers criminal intimidation and related offences.
4. **Consumer forum or civil court.** Where the harassment causes loss, damages can be claimed, and a civil court can restrain the agents.
5. **Cyber crime complaint.** For morphed images, contact-list misuse or blackmail through lending apps, a complaint can be made on the national cyber crime reporting portal or to the cyber crime wing.

Keep screenshots, call recordings (where legally permissible), messages and the dates and times of each incident.

## When the loan is genuinely owed

Harassment does not erase the debt. If you owe money, options include:

- asking the lender for **restructuring** or a longer tenure;
- negotiating a **one-time settlement (OTS)** for a reduced amount;
- clearing the dues in instalments with written confirmation; or
- defending a recovery suit or a cheque-bounce complaint if the claim is wrong or inflated.

## How to document a settlement

A settlement is only safe if it is written and followed by clear proof of closure. Make sure that:

1. The **settlement letter** states the exact amount, the date by which it is to be paid and that it is a full and final settlement of the account.
2. You pay through a **traceable banking channel**, never in cash to an individual agent.
3. You obtain a **no-dues certificate** or loan closure letter once the payment is made.
4. The lender confirms that it will **update the credit information companies** (such as CIBIL). A settled account is reported differently from a "closed" account, and this affects future credit.
5. Any **cheques** or other security given for the loan are returned.

## How a lawyer can help

A lawyer can send a legal notice asking the lender and its agents to stop unlawful conduct, review settlement terms before you sign, represent you in civil and criminal proceedings and file complaints with the regulators. A lawyer cannot make a valid debt disappear, and should not promise to.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a recovery agent come to my house?
Agents may visit at reasonable hours but cannot threaten or use force, enter without consent or embarrass you before neighbours.

### Can the lender call my relatives?
They may be contacted only for tracing you in limited ways; shaming you or discussing your debt with them is not allowed.

### Will a settlement hurt my credit score?
Yes. A settled account is usually reported as "settled" and can reduce your score. A full repayment is viewed more favourably.

### Is non-payment of a loan a criminal offence?
Not in itself. It becomes criminal only if there is fraud, a dishonoured cheque or similar offence.

### What if the lender is an unregistered app?
Report it to the police and the cyber crime portal. Unregistered digital lenders are not authorised to operate, and their recovery methods can be prosecuted.
