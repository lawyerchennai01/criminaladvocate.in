There is **no standard duration** for a forgery case in India. Many cases take **several years** from the FIR to a final judgment, mostly because of the forensic examination of the disputed document and the usual delays of the criminal justice system. This page explains the stages and the factors that make a forgery case shorter or longer, without promising any timeline.

## What forgery means in law

Under the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)**, the chapter on offences relating to documents (Sections 335 to 342, replacing Sections 463 to 477A of the IPC) covers:

- making a **false document** or false electronic record with intent to cause damage, support a claim or commit fraud;
- **forgery**, which is the making of a false document with intent to cause damage or injury, or to support a claim, or to commit fraud;
- forgery of a **valuable security or a will**, which carries heavier punishment;
- **possessing** a forged document, and **using it as genuine**; and
- **forging a seal or a record of a court or public register**.

Forgery is frequently charged together with **cheating** and **criminal conspiracy**, especially in property and loan matters.

## Stages of a forgery case

1. **Complaint or FIR.** Property, banking and employment disputes are the commonest sources.
2. **Investigation.** The police collect the disputed documents, admitted writings or signatures, bank records and registrar records.
3. **Forensic examination.** The disputed signature or writing is compared with specimen and admitted writings by a **handwriting expert** or a government forensic laboratory, and the report is received. This stage is often the main cause of delay.
4. **Charge sheet and cognizance.** The final report is filed and the court takes cognizance.
5. **Charges and trial.** Prosecution witnesses, including the complainant, officials and the expert, are examined.
6. **Judgment and appeal.** The accused is acquitted or convicted, and appeals follow.

## What affects the time taken

- **Backlog in forensic laboratories** and delay in getting the expert's opinion.
- **Number of documents and accused.** Multiple documents and several accused increase the number of witnesses.
- **Availability of original documents.** Missing or damaged originals create further disputes.
- **Parallel civil proceedings.** Title or ownership suits on the same property can proceed alongside, although a criminal case is not automatically stopped by the civil case.
- **Challenges in the High Court.** Petitions to quash the FIR, or transfers, can add time.
- **Absence of witnesses or the accused**, resulting in adjournments.

## Ways in which a case may end sooner

- **Quashing.** Where the dispute is essentially civil, or the allegations do not make out forgery, the High Court may quash the FIR under Section 528 of the BNSS.
- **Discharge.** The court may discharge the accused if the material does not support the charge.
- **Settlement.** Offences of forgery are not compoundable in general, but a genuine settlement of a private dispute can support a quashing petition, depending on the facts and the offence.

## What a person facing a forgery allegation should do

1. **Do not destroy or alter** any document.
2. **Collect the originals and copies** of the documents, including those showing the source of your signatures and the transaction history.
3. **Seek advice before giving specimen handwriting** or signatures, as these are used for comparison.
4. **Consider anticipatory bail** if arrest is likely.
5. **Explore whether the dispute is civil**, since courts do not allow criminal law to be used merely to pressure a party in a civil matter.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is forgery a bailable offence?
It depends on the section. Forgery for the purpose of cheating or of valuable securities is generally non-bailable, though bail is commonly considered.

### Can I be forced to give a handwriting sample?
A Magistrate can direct a person to give specimen writing or signature for comparison, and refusal can lead to adverse consequences.

### How reliable is a handwriting expert's opinion?
It is only an opinion, and courts need corroboration and look at the reasons given. It is often tested through cross-examination.

### Can a civil case and a forgery case go on together?
Yes. The standards of proof differ, and one does not automatically stop the other.

### Does a forgery conviction lead to imprisonment?
It can, depending on the section, with terms ranging up to life imprisonment for forgery of certain valuable securities.
