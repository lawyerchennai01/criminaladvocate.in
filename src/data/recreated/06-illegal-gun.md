If you are caught with an illegal firearm in India, you can be arrested without a warrant and prosecuted under the **Arms Act, 1959**. Possessing, carrying or acquiring a firearm or ammunition without a valid licence is a serious offence, and possession of "prohibited arms" or ammunition carries heavier penalties. The case is investigated by the police, tried in a criminal court, and the arms are sent to a forensic laboratory.

## What the Arms Act prohibits

- **Section 3:** no person may acquire, possess or carry any firearm or ammunition without a licence.
- **Section 5 and 7:** manufacture, sale and the possession of "prohibited arms" or ammunition require special permission.
- **Section 25:** punishments for possession or acquisition in contravention of the Act.
- **Section 27:** punishment for the *use* of arms or ammunition in contravention of the Act, with higher penalties for prohibited arms.

For possession of a firearm without a licence, the imprisonment is generally not less than **three years**, extendable to **seven years**, with a fine. For prohibited arms or ammunition, the minimum term is higher and can extend up to life imprisonment. Penalties were amended in 2019, so the current wording of the Act should always be checked.

## What happens after you are caught

1. **Arrest and seizure.** The police prepare a seizure mahazar in the presence of independent witnesses, seal the weapon and register an FIR.
2. **Production before the Magistrate.** You must be produced within twenty-four hours and remanded or released.
3. **Forensic examination.** The weapon goes to the ballistics laboratory, which reports whether it is a "firearm" in working condition, and whether any ammunition found is live.
4. **Previous sanction.** Section 39 of the Arms Act bars a prosecution for most offences under the Act unless the District Magistrate has given previous sanction. A prosecution without a valid sanction can fail.
5. **Charge sheet and trial.** After the charge sheet, the case goes to trial, and the prosecution must prove possession and the nature of the weapon.

## Grounds of defence that are often examined

- **Possession.** Whether the accused had actual or conscious control of the weapon, or whether it was merely found at a place accessible to others.
- **Seizure.** Whether the recovery was made in the presence of independent witnesses and the seizure documents were properly prepared.
- **Forensic proof.** Whether the object is a firearm within the Act, whether it was in working order, and whether the chain of custody is intact.
- **Valid licence.** If a licence existed but lapsed, or the weapon was held under a licence that covered it, the case can look different.
- **Sanction.** Absence of a valid sanction for prosecution under Section 39.

## Bail

Bail depends on the weapon, the quantity of ammunition, any linked offences and the accused's history. In straightforward possession cases without violence, courts frequently grant bail with conditions such as surrender of passport and reporting to the police. Where the offence is connected to a serious crime, bail is harder.

## Other laws that may apply

If the weapon is linked to a threat, a robbery or a death, charges under the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita for criminal intimidation, attempt to murder or murder can be added. Possession of explosives, or arms of a banned category, can also attract special laws.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is anticipatory bail available in an Arms Act case?
Yes, the remedy exists in law, but courts consider the nature of the weapon and the involvement of the accused before granting it.

### Is an air gun treated as a firearm?
That depends on its muzzle energy and design as defined in the Act and the Rules. A forensic opinion often decides this.

### I found an old weapon at home. What should I do?
Do not use or move it. Surrender it to the police station or arms authority, and take legal advice before doing so.

### Can a licence holder be prosecuted?
Yes, if the weapon is carried outside the licence terms, the licence has lapsed, or ammunition exceeds the permitted quantity.

### Who gives the sanction for prosecution?
The District Magistrate, under Section 39 of the Act.
