**Adultery is no longer a crime in India.** In *Joseph Shine v. Union of India* (2018), the Supreme Court struck down Section 497 of the Indian Penal Code, and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 does not contain an offence of adultery. However, an extramarital affair continues to matter in **civil and family law**: it can be a **ground for divorce**, can influence **maintenance** and can be relevant in **custody** and in other proceedings.

## Adultery as a ground for divorce

- Under the **Hindu Marriage Act, 1955**, a spouse can seek divorce if the other has had voluntary sexual intercourse with a person other than the spouse after the marriage.
- The **Special Marriage Act, 1954** and personal laws of other communities provide similar grounds in their own terms.
- A spouse can also rely on **cruelty**, which in some cases is established by a spouse's conduct outside the marriage, depending on its effect on the other spouse.

The burden is on the spouse who alleges adultery, and the court needs reliable proof. Suspicion is not enough.

## Effect on maintenance

A wife who is **living in adultery** is not entitled to maintenance under Section 144 of the BNSS (earlier Section 125 CrPC), and the husband may use this ground to oppose the claim, though it must be proved. In proceedings under personal laws, the conduct of the parties is also considered. A husband's own affair does not disqualify him from paying maintenance, and the court may consider his conduct while deciding custody and the financial settlement.

## Custody and children

Courts decide custody on the **welfare of the child**. An extramarital relationship by a parent is not by itself a bar to custody, but conduct that harms the child's welfare, such as neglect or exposure to unsuitable environments, can matter.

## Other legal issues linked to affairs

- **Bigamy.** A second marriage during the subsistence of the first is an offence (Section 82 of the BNS), independent of adultery.
- **Cheating or harassment.** If a person is deceived into a relationship, or if threats, stalking or blackmail occur, criminal laws on those offences apply.
- **Defamation.** Public allegations of an affair can lead to defamation claims.
- **Privacy.** Secretly recording calls, accessing a spouse's phone or placing tracking tools can raise privacy and cyber law issues.

## What evidence courts accept

- Photographs, messages and call records, **if lawfully obtained and properly proved**. Electronic evidence requires a **certificate** under the Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (earlier Section 65B of the Evidence Act).
- Hotel records, travel records and witness testimony.
- Admissions made by the spouse.

Evidence obtained through illegal access to accounts or devices can create problems for the person who collected it.

## Practical steps

1. **Do not confront or threaten.** Threats can create criminal liability for you.
2. **Preserve information** but avoid hacking or unlawful surveillance.
3. **Consider mediation or counselling**, which courts often encourage before a divorce.
4. **Take advice on financial and custody consequences** before filing or responding.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can my spouse file a criminal case against the other person?
Not for adultery. Other offences such as cheating, stalking or blackmail can still be reported.

### Can I get divorce on the ground of my spouse's affair?
Yes, if you can prove it. The petition is filed in the Family Court with the evidence.

### Does an affair affect child custody?
It is only one factor. The court's focus is the child's welfare.

### Can I record my spouse's phone calls?
Recording without consent can violate privacy and may not be admissible. Seek legal advice before doing so.

### Is adultery a ground for divorce for both husband and wife?
Yes, the ground is available to either spouse under the main marriage laws.
