**Contempt of court** means disobeying a court's order, or lowering the authority of the court or interfering with the administration of justice. In Tamil it is called **நீதிமன்ற அவமதிப்பு** (*neethimandra avamathippu*). The law in India is the Contempt of Courts Act, 1971, together with Articles 129 and 215 of the Constitution, which make the Supreme Court and the High Courts courts of record with the power to punish for their own contempt.

## நீதிமன்ற அவமதிப்பு என்றால் என்ன?

நீதிமன்றத்தின் தீர்ப்பு, உத்தரவு அல்லது அளித்த உறுதிமொழியை வேண்டுமென்றே மீறுவது **சிவில் அவமதிப்பு** ஆகும். நீதிமன்றத்தின் மதிப்பைக் குறைப்பது, நீதி நிர்வாகத்தில் குறுக்கிடுவது அல்லது நடைபெறும் வழக்கின் நியாயமான நடைமுறையைப் பாதிப்பது **குற்றவியல் அவமதிப்பு** ஆகும். இரண்டு வகையிலும் தண்டனை வழங்கும் அதிகாரம் உயர்நீதிமன்றத்திற்கு உண்டு.

## The two kinds of contempt

**Civil contempt** is wilful disobedience of a judgment, decree, direction, order or writ of a court, or wilful breach of an undertaking given to a court. The word *wilful* matters: if a party could not comply, or the order was unclear, that can be a defence.

**Criminal contempt** covers any act or publication that:

- scandalises, or tends to scandalise, or lowers the authority of any court;
- prejudices, or interferes with, any judicial proceeding; or
- interferes with, or obstructs, the administration of justice in any other manner.

## Punishment

Under Section 12 of the Act, contempt can be punished with simple imprisonment of up to **six months**, or a fine of up to **₹2,000**, or both. The court may discharge the accused or remit the punishment if a *bona fide* apology is made. An apology is not accepted merely because it is offered; the court looks at whether it is genuine and made at the right stage.

## Limitation

Proceedings for contempt must be started within **one year** from the date of the alleged contempt (Section 20).

## What is not contempt

- **Fair and accurate reports** of judicial proceedings.
- **Fair criticism** of a judicial act after the case has been heard and finally decided, as long as it does not impute motive or attack the court's integrity.
- **Truth as a defence**, where the justification is in the public interest and the request to rely on it is *bona fide*.
- **Innocent publication** of matter without reasonable grounds to believe it would interfere with proceedings.

## How a civil contempt petition proceeds in the Madras High Court

1. The petitioner shows a *clear order*, proof that the respondent had knowledge of it, and proof of non-compliance.
2. The court issues notice to the respondent, who must file a response, usually with an affidavit explaining what has been done.
3. Many matters end when the respondent complies, or "purges" the contempt, and tenders an unconditional apology or explanation.
4. If the court is not satisfied, it frames charges and may punish, or may give a last opportunity to comply.

## If you receive a contempt notice

- Read the order allegedly disobeyed and identify exactly what it required and by when.
- Collect proof of compliance, or of the reasons compliance was not possible.
- File a considered reply or counter-affidavit within the time given. Ignoring the notice is the worst option.
- Do not repeat the conduct complained of, and avoid public comment on the case.

## Frequently asked questions

### What is the difference between civil and criminal contempt?
Civil contempt is disobedience of a court order or undertaking. Criminal contempt is conduct that scandalises the court, prejudices a judicial proceeding or obstructs the course of justice.

### Is an unconditional apology always accepted?
No. The court decides whether the apology is sincere. A grudging or late apology may be rejected.

### Can a government officer be punished for contempt?
Yes. Officials who wilfully disobey a court order can be proceeded against, and courts commonly direct personal appearance of the officer concerned.

### Can I be punished for criticising a judgment on social media?
Fair criticism of a decided case is permitted. Allegations against the integrity of a judge or the institution, or comments on a pending case that interfere with it, can be treated as criminal contempt.

### How long do I have to file a contempt petition?
One year from the date of the alleged contempt. For continuing disobedience, courts have looked at the facts of each case.
