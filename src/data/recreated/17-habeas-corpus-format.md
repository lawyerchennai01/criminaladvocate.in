A **habeas corpus petition** asks the High Court to order that a person who is detained be **produced before the court** so that the legality of the detention can be examined. The Latin phrase means "you may have the body". In the Madras High Court it is filed under **Article 226 of the Constitution** and is registered as a **Habeas Corpus Petition (H.C.P.)**. It is among the quickest remedies against unlawful detention, because courts treat it as a matter of personal liberty under Article 21.

## When a habeas corpus petition is used

- A person is **detained without authority of law**, or not produced before a Magistrate within twenty-four hours of arrest.
- Detention continues after the **remand period has ended** or the bail has been granted.
- A person is **detained under a preventive detention law**, such as the Tamil Nadu Prevention of Dangerous Activities Act, 1982 (the "Goondas Act"), and the detention order or its grounds are defective.
- A person is **missing or confined** by family or others against their will, including adult persons whose whereabouts are not known.
- A child is allegedly held in unlawful custody, though courts often treat custody as a welfare question.

## Who can file

The detained person, a relative, a friend or any person acting in good faith on the detenu's behalf. The **detenu's relative** usually files when the person is in custody. The respondents are the State, usually through the **Home Secretary**, the **Commissioner or Superintendent of Police** and the **Superintendent of the prison** or the detaining authority.

## Format of the petition

**1. Cause title**

> IN THE HIGH COURT OF JUDICATURE AT MADRAS
> (Habeas Corpus Jurisdiction)
> H.C.P. No. ______ of 2026
> *Name of petitioner* … Petitioner
> Vs.
> 1. The State rep. by its Secretary to Government, Home Department
> 2. The Commissioner of Police / Superintendent of Police
> 3. The Superintendent, Central Prison … Respondents

**2. Heading**

"Petition under Article 226 of the Constitution of India praying for the issuance of a writ of habeas corpus calling for the records relating to the detention of ___, and directing the respondents to produce the said person before this Hon'ble Court and set him at liberty."

**3. Affidavit**

A supporting affidavit sworn by the petitioner states the relationship to the detenu, the date, time and place of detention, the facts of the case, and that the detention is illegal.

**4. Grounds**

Specific points, for example: the detenu was not informed of the grounds of arrest; was not produced before a Magistrate within twenty-four hours; was held beyond the remand period; the detention order was passed without application of mind; the grounds were not supplied in a language the detenu understands; or the representation was not considered promptly.

**5. Prayer**

The relief sought: issue a writ of habeas corpus to produce the detenu, declare the detention illegal and set the detenu at liberty, and pass any other order.

**6. Documents**

- Copy of the detention order and the grounds of detention, if available.
- Copy of the FIR, remand order or arrest memo.
- Representation made to the authorities.
- Proof of relationship and identity.
- Vakalatnama.

## Process in the Madras High Court

Habeas corpus petitions are listed **urgently**, usually before a Division Bench. The court calls on the State to produce the person and file a counter. For preventive detention, the court checks whether the **procedure was followed and the statutory timelines were met**, and whether the grounds are vague, stale or unrelated to public order.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a court fee payable?
A nominal court fee applies, and urgent listing is available on request with reasons.

### Can I file it myself?
A petitioner can appear in person, but a lawyer is advisable because the petition must set out the legal grounds precisely.

### Does the court always order release?
No. If the detention is lawful, the petition is dismissed. The court examines the record to decide.

### What if the detenu has been released by the time of hearing?
The petition may be closed as infructuous, though compensation or directions are sometimes considered.

### Is habeas corpus used for adult persons who have eloped?
Yes, parents sometimes file it. The court usually meets the adult person and allows them to decide where to live.
