**Money laundering** under the Prevention of Money Laundering Act, 2002 (PMLA) means knowingly being involved in any process or activity connected with the **proceeds of crime**, and projecting them as untainted property. The Act is enforced by the **Enforcement Directorate (ED)**. A money laundering case depends on an underlying "scheduled offence", such as cheating, corruption, a drug offence or forgery, which has generated the proceeds.

## Key provisions

- **Section 3:** defines the offence of money laundering.
- **Section 4:** punishment, generally rigorous imprisonment of three to seven years and fine; the term can extend to ten years where the scheduled offence is under the NDPS Act.
- **Section 5:** provisional attachment of property believed to be proceeds of crime.
- **Section 17 and 18:** search and seizure.
- **Section 19:** arrest, subject to safeguards.
- **Section 45:** conditions for bail.
- **Section 50:** the power to summon and record statements.

## How an ED case unfolds

1. **ECIR.** The ED registers an Enforcement Case Information Report, an internal record that is not shared like an FIR.
2. **Summons.** Persons are summoned under Section 50 to give statements and produce documents. Statements to the ED can be used in the case.
3. **Attachment.** Property can be attached provisionally and then confirmed by the **Adjudicating Authority**. Appeals go to the **Appellate Tribunal** under the PMLA.
4. **Arrest.** The ED may arrest if it has material to believe the person is guilty, and the reasons must be recorded in writing. The Supreme Court has held that the **grounds of arrest must be communicated to the arrested person in writing** (*Pankaj Bansal v. Union of India*, 2023).
5. **Complaint and trial.** The ED files a prosecution complaint before the **Special Court**.

## Bail under Section 45

Section 45 contains "twin conditions": the court must give the Public Prosecutor an opportunity to oppose the application and must be satisfied that there are **reasonable grounds for believing the accused is not guilty** and that they are **not likely to commit any offence while on bail**. The Supreme Court upheld these conditions in *Vijay Madanlal Choudhary v. Union of India* (2022). Certain categories, such as women, minors and the sick or infirm, are given a limited relaxation in the Act.

## Common defences

- **No scheduled offence.** If the underlying offence is quashed or the accused is acquitted, there are no proceeds of crime, and the ED case cannot continue.
- **Property is not tainted.** The property was acquired lawfully, long before the alleged offence, or is not derived from the alleged crime.
- **Procedural defects.** Non-compliance with Section 19, failure to share the grounds of arrest, or irregular attachment.
- **Lack of knowledge.** The accused was not knowingly involved in the process.
- **Challenging attachment** before the Adjudicating Authority and the Tribunal.

## Practical steps if you receive a summons

- Do not ignore it, but appear with an advocate's advice and carry the documents requested.
- Remember that **statements recorded under Section 50 can later be relied on**, so be accurate and consistent.
- Preserve bank records, property documents and correspondence.
- Consider anticipatory bail in appropriate cases, though courts apply the law strictly.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is the ED's ECIR the same as an FIR?
No. An ECIR is an internal document of the ED, and it is not provided to the accused as a matter of course.

### Can the ED arrest without registering an FIR?
The ED needs a scheduled offence on record, which is usually registered by the police or another agency.

### Can my property be attached before trial?
Yes, provisionally, if the ED believes it is proceeds of crime, subject to confirmation by the Adjudicating Authority.

### How is money laundering different from the underlying crime?
The underlying crime generates the proceeds. Money laundering is the dealing with those proceeds.

### Which court tries PMLA cases?
A Special Court designated under the Act.
