Section 110 of the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 allows an Executive Magistrate to require a person who is a *habitual offender* to execute a bond, with or without sureties, to keep good behaviour for a fixed period. It is a **preventive** provision: the person is not being punished for an offence, but is being asked to give security so that further offences are deterred. The same scheme continues in the chapter on security for keeping the peace and good behaviour in the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS).

## Who can be proceeded against under Section 110

The Magistrate must receive information that a person within the local limits of their jurisdiction falls into one of the listed categories. In broad terms these are persons who:

- are habitual robbers, house-breakers, thieves or forgers;
- habitually receive stolen property knowing it to be stolen, or habitually protect or harbour thieves;
- habitually commit, attempt to commit or abet offences such as kidnapping, abduction, extortion, cheating, mischief, or offences involving a breach of the peace;
- habitually commit or abet offences under certain special laws dealing with, for example, adulteration, the sale of drugs or the trafficking of persons; or
- are so desperate and dangerous that being at large without security is hazardous to the community.

The key word is **habitually**. A single case, or a handful of registered cases with no conviction and no consistent pattern, is generally not enough on its own. The Magistrate needs credible material, usually evidence of general repute and past conduct, that the person is a habitual offender.

## How the proceeding works

1. **Information and order.** The Magistrate records the substance of the information, the amount of the bond, the period (not more than three years) and the number and character of sureties.
2. **Summons or warrant.** If the person is not in custody, they are summoned or, in a suitable case, arrested by warrant to appear.
3. **Inquiry.** The Magistrate holds an inquiry that follows the procedure of a summons case. Witnesses are examined and the person can cross-examine them and lead evidence.
4. **Final order.** If the Magistrate is satisfied that security is necessary, they order the bond. If not, the person is discharged.
5. **Failure to furnish security.** The person can be detained for the bond period unless the security is given or the order is set aside.

## Section 110 compared with the Tamil Nadu Goondas Act

Section 110 and the Tamil Nadu Prevention of Dangerous Activities Act, 1982 are different tools. Section 110 asks for a *bond* from the person after an inquiry before an Executive Magistrate. The Goondas Act is a *preventive detention* law under which the detaining authority orders detention without trial and the remedy is usually a habeas corpus petition. Receiving a Section 110 notice does not mean detention has been ordered under the Goondas Act.

## Common grounds to contest the order

- The material does not show *habitual* conduct, only isolated or old cases.
- The case rests on police reports and hearsay, with no independent witness to general repute.
- The notice does not set out the substance of the information, so the person could not prepare a defence.
- The inquiry was conducted without giving a fair chance to cross-examine.
- The amount of the bond or the number of sureties is excessive for the person's means.

## Challenging an order

An order requiring security at the end of the inquiry can be appealed to the Court of Sessions. If a point of law or jurisdiction is involved, the High Court can also be approached through its revisional or inherent powers. Because the person may be held in custody until the security is furnished, a prompt application for an interim stay is often important.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Section 110 CrPC a criminal charge?
No. It is a preventive proceeding. The person is asked to give security for good behaviour; they are not convicted of any offence by the order.

### For how long can a good behaviour bond run?
The period cannot exceed three years from the date of the order.

### What happens if the bond is breached?
If the person commits an offence during the bond period, the bond can be forfeited and the person may be proceeded against for the offence. The amount of the bond is then payable.

### Can pending criminal cases alone justify a Section 110 order?
Pending cases are relevant, but the Magistrate must still be satisfied from evidence that the person is a habitual offender. Courts have set aside orders that rest only on the number of registered cases.

### Who hears Section 110 matters in Chennai?
They are heard by an Executive Magistrate, such as the Sub-Divisional Magistrate or other officers with the same powers, not by the criminal courts that try offences.
