The **Border Security Force (BSF)** is a central armed police force under the Ministry of Home Affairs and is governed by the **Border Security Force Act, 1968** and the **BSF Rules, 1969**. Its members are subject to **force discipline** and, depending on the allegation, may also be answerable before ordinary criminal courts. This page outlines the routes available when someone wishes to raise allegations against a BSF officer.

## Which route fits which allegation

| Nature of allegation | Usual route |
|---|---|
| Misconduct, indiscipline, abuse of authority | Complaint to the officer's unit or higher force authorities |
| A criminal offence against a civilian, such as assault, theft or sexual offence | FIR with the local police, and complaint to senior force authorities |
| Violation of human rights | Complaint to the National Human Rights Commission or the State Human Rights Commission |
| Workplace sexual harassment | Complaint to the Internal Complaints Committee under the POSH Act, 2013 |
| Denial of fundamental rights | Writ petition in the High Court under Article 226 |

## Complaint within the force

A complaint can be sent to the **Commandant of the unit**, the **Deputy Inspector General** of the sector or frontier, and, if needed, the **Inspector General and the Director General** of the BSF. For serious complaints, a copy may also be sent to the **Ministry of Home Affairs**. Provide dates, places, names, a clear narration and any supporting material, and keep proof of delivery.

The force may order a **preliminary inquiry** or a **Court of Inquiry**, and, where the evidence warrants it, **disciplinary or trial proceedings** under the BSF Act.

## FIR against a BSF member

If the allegation involves a **cognizable offence**, a complaint can be made to the local police station. The police can register an FIR irrespective of the accused's service status. Under the BSF Act, an offence by a member of the force that is also an offence under ordinary law can, depending on the circumstances, be tried either by an ordinary criminal court or by a **Security Force Court**, and the Act provides a mechanism to decide which forum proceeds. Prosecution of a public servant for acts in the discharge of official duty may also require **sanction**, which makes the facts important.

## Human rights bodies and courts

- **NHRC and State Human Rights Commission.** For armed forces, the commission's role is generally limited to seeking a report from the Central Government and making recommendations.
- **High Court.** A writ petition can seek an inquiry, compensation or other relief where a right has been violated.
- **Civil and criminal courts.** A victim can also seek compensation and prosecution through ordinary law.

## Practical tips

1. **Write down** what happened as soon as possible, with times, places and names.
2. **Preserve evidence:** photographs, medical records, messages and witness details.
3. **Report in writing** and keep an acknowledgement.
4. **Avoid defamatory public statements** while the matter is under inquiry.
5. **Seek legal advice early** because questions of jurisdiction, sanction and limitation can decide which remedy works.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can a civilian file an FIR against a BSF jawan or officer?
Yes, for a cognizable offence. The police can register it, and jurisdiction questions between the civil court and the Security Force Court are then examined.

### What is a Security Force Court?
It is a court constituted under the BSF Act to try members of the force for offences under the Act and certain other offences.

### Can the NHRC investigate the BSF directly?
Its powers regarding armed forces are limited. It usually calls for a report from the Central Government.

### Is there a time limit?
The BSF Act and ordinary criminal law have their own limitation rules, and delays can weaken a complaint. Act promptly.

### What if the officer is from another state or posted at the border?
The location of the incident decides which police station and court have jurisdiction, and the force's disciplinary route stays available.
