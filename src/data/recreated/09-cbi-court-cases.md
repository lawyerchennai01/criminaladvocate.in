The **Central Bureau of Investigation (CBI)** investigates serious offences such as corruption by public servants, bank frauds and economic offences, mostly under the Prevention of Corruption Act, 1988 (PC Act) and the Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023. Cases that the CBI registers are tried by a **Special Judge** designated under the PC Act, and in Chennai these matters are heard in the special court for CBI cases. This page explains the stages of a CBI case and what an accused person should understand about each.

## How a CBI case begins

The CBI registers a **Regular Case (RC)** after a source information report, a preliminary enquiry, a complaint, or on the direction of a court. For public servants, **Section 17A** of the PC Act (introduced in 2018) generally requires prior approval of the competent authority before an enquiry or investigation into decisions taken in the discharge of official functions, except in cases of arrest on the spot for accepting a bribe.

## Offences commonly charged

| Offence | Provision |
|---|---|
| Public servant taking an undue advantage (bribe) | Section 7 PC Act, imprisonment from three up to seven years and fine |
| Criminal misconduct, including disproportionate assets | Section 13 PC Act, imprisonment from four up to ten years and fine |
| Criminal conspiracy, cheating and forgery in bank fraud cases | Provisions of the BNS, often with the PC Act |
| Money laundering | Prosecuted separately by the Enforcement Directorate under the PMLA |

## Stages of the case

1. **Search and arrest.** The CBI may search premises under a warrant and arrest an accused, who must be produced before the Special Judge within twenty-four hours.
2. **Remand and custody.** The court decides whether to grant CBI custody and for how long.
3. **Bail.** For offences under the PC Act, bail is decided on the facts: the nature of allegations, the evidence, the risk of tampering and the period of custody.
4. **Charge sheet and sanction.** Before the court takes cognizance against a public servant, **previous sanction for prosecution** under Section 19 of the PC Act is generally required from the competent authority. A defect in sanction is a legal ground of defence.
5. **Framing of charges.** The accused can apply for discharge if the material does not support the charge.
6. **Trial.** Prosecution witnesses, documents and forensic or audit reports are examined, followed by the statement of the accused and defence evidence.
7. **Judgment and appeal.** An appeal from the Special Judge lies to the High Court.

## Typical grounds raised by the defence

- Absence of valid sanction, or sanction given without application of mind.
- Failure to prove **demand and acceptance** of the bribe in trap cases, which the prosecution must establish.
- Procedural lapses in the **trap**, such as missing independent witnesses or irregular handling of currency.
- In disproportionate assets cases, errors in the computation of income, assets and the **check period**.
- Delay and prejudice in the investigation.

## What to consider when choosing an advocate

Rather than ranking advocates, which is not possible fairly, look at **fit**:

- Has the advocate **appeared before the Special Court** and the High Court in PC Act or bank fraud matters?
- Will they explain the sanction, bail and trial strategy in plain language?
- Do they give a **written fee agreement** and avoid promising a result?
- Can the team handle the documentary load, such as audit records and bank statements?

## Frequently asked questions

### Can the CBI arrest me without notice?
In a case where arrest is warranted, the CBI can arrest without a prior notice. You are entitled to be informed of the grounds of arrest and to consult a lawyer.

### Is sanction always required?
For prosecution of a serving public servant under the PC Act, previous sanction is generally required. It is not required in some circumstances, such as for a public servant who has ceased to hold the relevant office in a way the law recognises.

### Can I get anticipatory bail in a CBI case?
It can be sought from the Special Court or the High Court, which weigh the allegations and the need for custodial interrogation.

### How long does a CBI trial take?
There is no fixed time. Large documentary cases can take years.

### What is the difference between the CBI and the Enforcement Directorate?
The CBI investigates predicate offences such as corruption. The Enforcement Directorate investigates money laundering under the PMLA. A person can face both.
