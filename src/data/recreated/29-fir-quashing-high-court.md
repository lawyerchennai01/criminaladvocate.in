If an FIR has been registered against you, and it discloses no offence or is an abuse of process, you can ask the **Madras High Court** to **quash** it. The petition is filed under **Section 528 of the Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023** (earlier Section 482 of the CrPC), which preserves the High Court's inherent power to prevent abuse of process and secure the ends of justice. This page gives a practical overview of the grounds, the process and what to expect.

## Grounds on which FIRs are challenged

- The facts in the FIR, taken as true, **do not constitute the offence alleged**.
- The matter is a **civil or commercial dispute** that has been given a criminal colour, such as a payment dispute presented as cheating.
- The complaint is **mala fide**, motivated by personal enmity or to pressure a party.
- The FIR is **delayed** without explanation and is inherently improbable.
- There is a **legal bar** to the proceeding, such as the lack of a required sanction or complaint by a competent authority.
- The parties have **settled** a private dispute (see the separate article on compromise-based quashing).
- The same allegations have already been **investigated and closed**, and a fresh FIR is an abuse.

## How the petition is filed

1. **Draft the petition** as a **Criminal Original Petition (Crl.O.P.)** under Section 528 BNSS, with the facts, the legal grounds and the prayer to quash the FIR (and any consequential proceedings).
2. **Attach documents**: the FIR, the complaint, relevant correspondence or agreements, and any previous court orders.
3. **File in the Madras High Court** (or the Madurai Bench where the case falls within its jurisdiction), with an affidavit and vakalatnama.
4. **Seek interim relief** if there is urgency, such as a direction that **no coercive steps** be taken against the petitioner while the petition is pending.

## What happens at the hearing

- The court issues **notice to the State**, through the Public Prosecutor, and to the **de facto complainant**.
- The State files a **status report** on the investigation. The court may ask for the case diary to see whether the investigation is genuine.
- The court hears both sides on whether the allegations disclose a **cognizable offence** and whether the High Court should intervene.
- The court may **dismiss** the petition, **quash** the FIR, or **dispose of it** with directions, such as allowing the investigation to continue, but directing the police to follow a particular procedure.

## What the court usually does not do

The High Court does **not conduct a mini-trial**. It does not weigh evidence or decide disputed facts at this stage. It also hesitates to stop an investigation into a serious offence, so the strength of the petition lies in showing, on the face of the FIR and the admitted documents, that no offence is made out.

## Time and cost

There is no fixed timeline. The time depends on the court's list, whether interim relief is granted and whether the State files its status report on time. Fees are agreed with your advocate. See the separate note on how fee agreements and Bar Council rules apply.

## Practical tips

- **Move early.** Delay can reduce the force of a petition, and arrest may happen in the meantime.
- **Be accurate and complete.** Suppression of earlier proceedings can lead to dismissal and costs.
- **Cooperate with the investigation** while the petition is pending, unless the court orders otherwise.
- **Keep the documents ready** that show the dispute is civil in nature.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a quashing petition the same as anticipatory bail?
No. Anticipatory bail protects you from arrest. Quashing seeks to end the case itself. They can be filed together.

### Can I file the petition myself?
It is a technical proceeding, and advocates normally handle it.

### What does "no coercive steps" mean?
It is an interim direction that the police must not arrest or take harsh action while the petition is pending.

### Can the complainant oppose?
Yes. The complainant is heard and may point out why the matter should proceed.

### What if the petition is dismissed?
You may appeal to the Supreme Court by special leave, or pursue other remedies such as bail and discharge.
