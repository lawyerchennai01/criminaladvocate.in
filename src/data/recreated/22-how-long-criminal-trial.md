There is **no fixed duration** for a criminal trial in India. A simple summons case before a Magistrate may finish in months, while a serious Sessions Court trial with many witnesses can take several years. The actual time depends on the offence, the number of accused and witnesses, the court's workload and whether the case is delayed by adjournments or higher court proceedings. This page explains the stages so that you can understand where time is spent.

## Stages of a typical criminal case

1. **Complaint and FIR.** The case begins with an FIR or a private complaint.
2. **Investigation.** The police collect evidence, record statements and make arrests. A charge sheet must be filed within **60 or 90 days** of the start of custody for the accused to be denied default bail, depending on the offence.
3. **Cognizance and supply of documents.** The court takes cognizance of the charge sheet and the accused receives copies of the documents.
4. **Committal (Sessions cases).** For offences triable only by the Sessions Court, the Magistrate commits the case.
5. **Framing of charges.** After hearing both sides, the court frames charges or discharges the accused.
6. **Prosecution evidence.** Witnesses are examined and cross-examined. This is usually the longest stage.
7. **Statement of the accused.** The accused is asked to explain the incriminating evidence.
8. **Defence evidence.** The defence may lead evidence, though it is not obliged to.
9. **Final arguments.** Both sides argue and file written submissions.
10. **Judgment.** The court acquits or convicts. If convicted, there is a hearing on sentence.
11. **Appeal or revision.** The decision can be challenged in a higher court.

## Why trials take time

- **Witnesses not appearing** or being untraceable, so that dates are repeatedly adjourned.
- **Large numbers of accused and witnesses**, especially in conspiracy, financial and organised crime cases.
- **Forensic and expert reports** that are delayed.
- **Court workload and vacancies**, which cause long gaps between hearings.
- **Interim proceedings**, such as petitions for quashing, transfer or bail in higher courts.
- **Absence of the accused or counsel**, which requires fresh dates and processes.

## What the law says about speed

The right to a **speedy trial** is part of Article 21 of the Constitution, recognised in decisions such as *Hussainara Khatoon v. State of Bihar* (1979). The **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023** also introduces timelines for several steps, limits on adjournments and provision for electronic proceedings. Special laws such as POCSO set targets for trial completion. These timelines guide the courts but are not always met in practice.

## Protection for undertrial prisoners

A person detained during the trial has some legal protection against prolonged custody:

- **Default bail** if the investigation is not completed within the statutory time.
- **Release after serving a part of the maximum sentence.** Section 479 of the BNSS allows an undertrial who has served one-third of the maximum sentence (if a first-time offender) or one-half (in other cases) to be released on bond. It does not apply to offences punishable with death or life imprisonment, or where several cases are pending against the person.
- **Bail on the ground of delay**, which courts have granted where the trial is unreasonably prolonged.

## What you can do to avoid avoidable delay

- **Attend every hearing**, and keep your advocate informed of changes of address.
- **Provide documents and witness details** promptly.
- **Avoid unnecessary adjournments**; courts increasingly record reasons for them.
- **Consider compounding or settlement**, if the offence permits.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I get a case closed faster by pleading guilty?
A plea of guilty is a serious decision with legal consequences, and the court may still examine whether it is voluntary. Take advice before doing so.

### How long does a cheque bounce case take?
Section 138 cases are meant to be tried summarily, but they often take longer than intended.

### Does a High Court stay stop the trial?
A stay of the trial proceedings pauses it until the High Court decides the petition.

### Is there a time limit for a trial to end?
Not in general. Some special laws set targets, and the Constitution protects the right to a speedy trial.

### Can an accused ask for a speedy trial?
Yes. An application for a speedy trial, or a petition in the High Court, can be made where delay is causing prejudice.
