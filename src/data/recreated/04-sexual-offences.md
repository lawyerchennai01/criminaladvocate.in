Sexual offences in India are defined and punished under the **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)**, which replaced the Indian Penal Code, and, where the victim is a child, under the **Protection of Children from Sexual Offences (POCSO) Act, 2012**. This page explains in plain language how these cases work for both survivors and persons accused, and where to find help.

## The main offences under the BNS

| Offence | BNS section (indicative) |
|---|---|
| Rape (definition) and its punishment | Sections 63 and 64 |
| Rape in specified aggravated situations, including of girls below sixteen or twelve | Section 65 |
| Rape causing death or a persistent vegetative state | Section 66 |
| Gang rape | Section 70 |
| Disclosing the identity of a victim | Section 72 |
| Sexual harassment | Section 75 |
| Assault or use of force to disrobe a woman | Section 76 |
| Voyeurism | Section 77 |
| Stalking | Section 78 |
| Word, gesture or act intended to insult the modesty of a woman | Section 79 |

Rape attracts rigorous imprisonment of not less than ten years, extendable to life, along with a fine, and higher minimum terms apply in aggravated situations. Penalties and section numbers should always be checked against the current text of the statute.

## How a case begins

1. **Complaint and FIR.** A cognizable offence must be registered by the police. A "Zero FIR" can be registered at any police station and then transferred to the station with jurisdiction.
2. **Medical examination.** A survivor is entitled to a medical examination by a registered medical practitioner with her consent.
3. **Statement before a Magistrate.** The survivor's statement may be recorded before a Judicial Magistrate under Section 183 of the BNSS (earlier Section 164 CrPC).
4. **Investigation and charge sheet.** The police collect medical, forensic and witness evidence and file a report before the court.
5. **Trial.** Trials for rape are held in-camera, and the court is expected to take steps to protect the dignity and identity of the survivor. Cases involving children are heard by Special Courts under POCSO.

## Rights of a survivor

- To have a complaint recorded, ideally by a woman police officer.
- To free legal aid and a lawyer through the legal services authorities.
- To privacy: publishing the name or identity of the survivor is an offence.
- To compensation under the victim compensation scheme, which is available irrespective of the outcome of the trial in appropriate cases.

## Rights of a person accused

A person accused is presumed innocent until proven guilty. They have the right to be informed of the grounds of arrest, to consult a lawyer of their choice, to be produced before a Magistrate within twenty-four hours, to seek bail and to a fair trial. Courts also recognise that sexual offence allegations can be false or exaggerated in some cases; the evidence, medical records, digital messages and the survivor's statement are examined carefully, and High Courts have powers to quash proceedings that are an abuse of process.

## Support and helplines

- **112:** the national emergency response number.
- **181:** the Women Helpline.
- **1098:** CHILDLINE, for children in need of care and protection.
- **District Legal Services Authority:** free legal aid for women, children and others who qualify under the Legal Services Authorities Act.

## Choosing legal support

Look for an advocate with direct experience of trials in the court where the case is pending, who explains the stages and possible outcomes honestly, and who does not guarantee a result. Ask for a written fee agreement. No ethical advocate can promise the result of a criminal case.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a delay in filing a complaint fatal to the case?
No. Courts recognise that survivors may delay for social or emotional reasons, but the explanation for the delay is weighed along with the other evidence.

### Can a rape case be compromised?
Rape is a serious non-compoundable offence. Courts generally do not quash it on compromise, although facts such as a long-standing relationship may be considered at the bail stage.

### What is a Zero FIR?
It is an FIR registered at any police station irrespective of where the offence happened. It is then transferred to the correct station.

### Is anticipatory bail available in a rape case?
It is available in law, but courts look closely at the nature of the allegations and the evidence, and may refuse it in serious cases.

### Where can I get free legal help in Chennai?
Through the Tamil Nadu State Legal Services Authority and the District Legal Services Authority offices located in the court complexes.
